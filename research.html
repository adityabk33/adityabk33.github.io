With Carlos Soto, Nathan Wycoff and Davide Sartor

Differential privacy on manifolds

With Carlos Soto · ICML 2026

Brain shapes, rotations and covariance matrices don't live in flat space; they live on curved manifolds. The usual way to make a statistic private is to add Euclidean noise, but that can push the answer off the manifold and produce something that isn't a valid shape or matrix at all.

We built differentially private geodesic regression, where both the model and the privacy noise are defined on the manifold itself. My part was the sensitivity analysis that sets how much noise is needed, which I derived using Jacobi fields so that the bound reflects the curvature of the space. We tested it on spheres, positive-definite matrices, Kendall's shape space, and corpus callosum shapes from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative.

Bayesian optimization for fine-tuning language models

With Carlos Soto, Nathan Wycoff and Davide Sartor · Preprint 2026

New parameterizations can adapt a large language model by learning only a handful of numbers. That leaves a small search space where every evaluation is expensive, which is exactly the setting Bayesian optimization is designed for.

LlMBO tunes those parameters using inference alone, with no backward passes. A Gaussian-process surrogate tracks how accurate each candidate is likely to be, and a trust region focuses the search. In very small adaptation spaces it matches or beats policy-gradient training (GRPO) on math reasoning benchmarks, while using substantially less compute and memory. I built the optimization and evaluation pipeline and designed the matched comparisons.

Privacy over Lie groups

In progress

Many datasets record rigid motions: rotations and translations of bodies, cameras or molecules. These form Lie groups, where the order of operations matters. I am extending intrinsic privacy to this setting, privatizing the group mean with noise built from the group's own structure, and with sensitivity bounds that account for non-commutativity through the Lie bracket.

Domain adaptation for astronomical images

Models trained on simulated galaxy images often stumble on real survey data because noise, blurring and redshift distributions differ. I used optimal-transport alignment to bridge that gap, improving galaxy morphology classification and gravitational-lens size estimates.

Theoretical physics

With Lorenzo Sorbo, UMass Amherst

Fermions produced by evolving axions

With Lorenzo Sorbo · JCAP 2025

Axions are hypothetical fields that could be the dark matter or drive cosmic acceleration. As an axion evolves, it can create particles and hand them some of its energy. If enough energy leaks out, the axion's predicted abundance changes.

I developed a framework to compute the spectrum of fermions produced this way, in both the radiation and matter eras. An oscillating axion produces both spin orientations almost equally. A slowly rolling one can strongly favor one of them, and heavy fermions produced this way can act as an extra matter component.

When gauge fields push back during inflation

With Lorenzo Sorbo · Preprint 2026

In axion inflation, the rolling inflaton amplifies gauge fields, and those fields drag on the inflaton in return. Because they respond with a delay, the motion can overshoot and oscillate rather than settle smoothly. This matters because the gravitational waves and magnetic fields it produces depend sharply on that history.

I built an analytical description of the transition from weak to strong backreaction. Expanding around a reference solution and using a Laplace transform, I predict growth rates, oscillation frequencies and amplitudes directly, and these predictions agree with full numerical solutions.

Read the preprint (arXiv)

Primordial black holes from inflation

Current work

Lattice simulations show that backreaction is not uniform across space. I am extending my analysis to these inhomogeneities to find how fluctuations are sourced, which scales they prefer, and when the uniform picture breaks down.

The payoff is observational. Gauge fields can boost density fluctuations on small scales, and large enough ones collapse into primordial black holes, a possible component of dark matter. I aim to pin down which predictions for black holes, gravitational waves and primordial magnetic fields hold up once backreaction's full structure is included.
